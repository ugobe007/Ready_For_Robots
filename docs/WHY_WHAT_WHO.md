# WHY → WHAT → WHO

**Status:** Product intelligence model (2026-08-15)  
**Builds on:** [robot_buying_journey.md](./robot_buying_journey.md) · [robot_job_v0_1.md](./robot_job_v0_1.md)

---

## Three layers (not one)

| Layer | Question | Content |
|-------|----------|---------|
| **WHY** | Why would this organization automate? | Pain / economics / capacity / labor / safety / expansion / continuity |
| **WHAT** | What physical work could be automated? | Robot Job (solution-neutral) |
| **WHO** | Who can automate it? | Robotability → class → vendor/robot fit |

ReadyForRobots has historically jumped toward **WHO** because we sell to robot companies.  
The intelligence advantage may sit in **WHY + WHAT**.

---

## Mapping to buyer stages

```
WHY:  Pain → Quantify → Alternatives considered
WHAT: Physical work defined (Robot Job)
WHO:  Tech uncertainty → Org risk → Economics → Pilot → Expand
```

---

## Two market faces (same engine)

### Face A — Robot seller
> We found work your robot can win.

Emphasizes: WHAT + WHO (fit) + enough WHY for timing.

### Face B — Automation buyer (operator)
> We found jobs in *your* operations that may be worth automating.

Emphasizes: WHY + WHAT.  
**Do not** lead with a vendor. Example shape:

```
JOB 1 — Dock → storage pallet movement
  Automation potential: HIGH
  Operational burden: HIGH
  Required capability: autonomous pallet transport
  Definition completeness: 67%

JOB 2 — Cycle counting
  Required capability: autonomous inventory scanning

JOB 3 — Case picking
  Required capability: mobile manipulation
```

Operator helps fill unknowns → **then** match vendors.

That is a different ReadyForRobots — and a hypothesis to test, not a homepage claim.

---

## Detection order (discipline)

1. Observe **pain / need** signals (WHY hypotheses).  
2. Investigate whether **physical work** can be stated (WHAT / Admission Test).  
3. Only then score **embodied classes and vendors** (WHO).

Never: hiring = lead = email Cal.
